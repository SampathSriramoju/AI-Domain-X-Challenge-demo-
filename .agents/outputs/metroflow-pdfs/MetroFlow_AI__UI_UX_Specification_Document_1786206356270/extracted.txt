1. Product Overview 
Vision 
To become the global standard for data-driven municipal traffic management, empowering 
every city to eliminate preventable gridlock through intelligent, explainable, and accessible 
signal timing optimization. 
PDF 
Objectives 
●​ Automate Bottleneck Detection: Reduce bottleneck identification time from weeks 
to under 30 seconds.​

●​ Optimize Green Split & Coordination: Generate offline signal timing plans that 
yield an estimated 12%-25% reduction in average intersection delay.​

●​ Explainable Recommendations: Provide plain-language narratives powered by AI 
for every recommended signal change.​
 PDF 
●​ Low-Barrier Simulation: Supply "before vs. after" queue and delay simulation 
estimates without requiring complex micro-simulation software.​

Users 
●​ Primary: Municipal Traffic Engineers, Traffic Operations Center (TOC) Managers.​

Scope 
MetroFlow AI is strictly an offline decision-support tool. It ingests historical traffic volume 
data, road metadata, and incident logs to automatically detect bottlenecks and compute 
optimal green-split adjustments.

7. Dashboard Design 
The dashboard must reflect premium enterprise SaaS analytics, avoiding cluttered visuals. 
●​ KPIs: Placed at the top for immediate executive visibility. Use large typography for 
numbers (e.g., 1.12 V/C) and smaller semantic text for units. 
●​ Cards: Use subtle shadows and rounded corners (Border radius: 8px) to contain 
discrete metrics (e.g., Delay Reduction). 
●​ Heatmaps/Maps: Use a dark-mode base map by default to allow brightly colored 
intersection nodes (Red = Bottleneck, Green = Free Flow) to stand out sharply.​
 PDF 
●​ Alerts/Status Indicators: Strict semantic coloring. Red for LOS F/Safety breaches, 
Yellow for LOS D-E, Green for LOS A-C. 
●​ Recommended Actions: Action buttons should be high-contrast and logically placed 
at the bottom right of the screen flow to guide the user to the next step. 
●​ Why every widget exists: To map directly to a user story, prioritizing rapid 
decision-making and defensible engineering reporting over decorative elements.​
 PDF 
8. Data Visualization Guide 
●​ Traffic Volume & Hourly Trend: Bar Chart with Overlay Box. The bar chart 
accurately reflects discrete hourly volume aggregation, while a shaded overlay 
clearly delineates the algorithmic clustering of the "Peak Window".​
 PDF+ 1 
●​ Queue Length & Delay: Side-by-Side Metric Delta Cards. The core value 
proposition is the "Before vs. After" comparison. A simple side-by-side layout with a 
percentage reduction badge is the fastest way to communicate ROI.​
 PDF 
●​ Congestion Severity (LOS) & V/C Ratio: Node-based GIS Heatmap. Traffic 
engineers think spatially. Mapping V/C ratios directly onto intersection nodes on a 
linear corridor instantly reveals where the system is failing (e.g., INT_04).​
 PDF+ 1 
●​ Signal Timing Comparison: Horizontal Stacked Bar Chart (Phase Diagram). 
Maps directly to industry-standard NEMA ring-and-barrier diagrams, showing how the 
total cycle length is sliced into green, yellow, and red intervals. 
●​ OPTIONAL HACKATHON ENHANCEMENT - Simulation / Progression: Time-Space 
Diagram. Shows distance on the Y-axis and time on the X-axis, allowing engineers to 
visually trace the green wave (platoon progression) across the arterial.​
 PDF+ 1

11. Accessibility 
●​ WCAG 2.1 AA Compliance: Minimum contrast ratio of 4.5:1 for all text against 
backgrounds. 
●​ Color Contrast: Critical UI elements (like bottlenecks) must not rely on color alone; 
use icons or text labels (e.g., explicitly stating "LOS F") alongside the red color. 
●​ Keyboard Navigation: Fully tab-navigable interface, prioritizing the sequential user 
journey flow. 
●​ Focus States: High-visibility blue focus rings (box-shadow: 0 0 0 3px 
rgba(15, 82, 186, 0.5)) around active sliders and inputs. 
●​ Dark Mode: Supported natively, as TOC command centers often operate in low-light 
environments.​

12. UX Details 
●​ Empty States: When a project is created but no CSV is uploaded, display an 
illustration of a traffic intersection with a clear "Upload CSV to Begin" prompt. 
●​ Loading States: Use skeleton screens matching the final layout (e.g., pulsing grey 
bars where the bar chart will appear) instead of generic spinners during the 5-second 
optimization calculation.​
 PDF 
●​ Error Handling: Inline validation during CSV upload. If turning ratios sum to > 1.0, 
highlight the specific cell in red and disable the "Analyze" button.​
 PDF 
●​ Toast Notifications: Non-intrusive bottom-right toasts for background actions (e.g., 
"NEMA CSV Exported Successfully"). 
13. Microinteractions 
●​ File Upload: Dropzone border dashes animate and background color dims slightly 
upon dragenter. 
●​ Charts: Bars in the hourly volume chart grow from the X-axis upon page load. 
●​ Simulation Slider: Moving the "What-If" volume surge slider causes the queue 
length numbers to smoothly tick up/down rather than flashing instantly, visually 
reinforcing the mathematical relationship.​
 PDF+ 1 
●​ Safety Lock: Attempting to drag a green split slider below the pedestrian minimum 
triggers a subtle horizontal "shake" animation on the slider handle, accompanied by a 
visual lock icon.​

16. Hackathon Demo Flow 
Ideal 5-Minute Script: 
1.​ Transition 1 (Screen 1 & 2 - 45 seconds): 
○​ Action: Presenter logs in and drags a CSV onto the upload zone. 
○​ Narration: "Cities waste months manually identifying traffic patterns. We just 
uploaded 7 days of raw count data for Main Street. MetroFlow AI instantly 
cleans and processes it." 
2.​ Transition 2 (Screen 3 - 60 seconds): 
○​ Action: Screen reveals the Corridor Heatmap. Presenter hovers over 
Intersection 4. 
○​ Narration: "Instantly, we see our AM Peak is 7:45 to 9:15, and our primary 
bottleneck is Intersection 4, failing with a V/C ratio of 1.12. Let's fix this."

○​ Action: Click to Optimization Workbench. Adjust the What-If slider. Attempt to 
reduce pedestrian time. 
○​ Narration: "Our offline engine applies Webster's method, redistributing green 
time to the saturated Eastbound approach. Watch as I drag the 'What-If' slider 
to simulate a 20% traffic surge—the model recalculates dynamically. If I try to 
reduce green time below the pedestrian minimum walk time... the system 
locks it. Safety is hardcoded." 
4.​ Transition 4 (Screen 5 - 60 seconds): 
○​ Action: Reveal the AI summary text and click the Export PDF button. 
○​ Narration: "Finally, we use Azure OpenAI to translate these complex queuing 
dynamics into a plain-language executive summary, explicit assumptions 
included. We export the NEMA controller file, and we're ready for field 
programming in under 5 minutes."

17. Judge Review 
Acting as a Hackathon Judge evaluating this UI/UX Specification: 
Weaknesses & Limitations 
●​ Reliance on Static CSVs: The UI relies on the user uploading clean, structured CSV 
data, whereas real municipal loop detector data is notoriously messy.​
 PDF 
●​ Queuing Model Limitations: UI must make it incredibly obvious that M/M/1 queuing 
equations under-predict delay during extreme oversaturated conditions (V/C>1.2).​
 PDF 
Missing Features / UI Considerations 
●​ Pedestrian & Bicycle Phasing: The UI heavily emphasizes vehicular throughput, 
but engineers legally require detailed interfaces for Flashing Don't Walk (FDW) and 
bicycle minimums.​
 PDF 
Potential Judge Questions 
●​ Question: "How do you ensure the AI doesn't hallucinate a dangerous signal timing?" 
●​ Answer: The UI and architecture clearly separate math from text. Python 
deterministic engines handle all mathematical generation and safety locks; Azure 
OpenAI solely reads a structured JSON to generate a narrative summary.​
 PDF+ 1 
Optional Enhancements Evaluated 
●​ Automated CSV Data Cleaning UI: Highly recommended to add a pre-analysis 
screen where users can visualize missing timestamps being interpolated.​
 PDF 
●​ Time-Space Progression Diagram: The inclusion of this diagram in the UI greatly 
elevates the engineering credibility of the platform.​
