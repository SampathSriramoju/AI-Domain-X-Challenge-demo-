1. Executive Summary
MetroFlow AI is an offline urban traffic analytics and signal timing optimization platform designed specifically for municipal
traffic engineers, smart city consultants, and urban transportation authorities. Urban intersections frequently suffer from
severe delay and congestion caused by outdated, static signal timing plans that fail to reflect modern traffic fluctuations.
MetroFlow AI ingests historical traffic volume data, road metadata, and incident logs to automatically detect bottleneck
patterns, compute optimal offline green-split and offset adjustments using queuing theory, and generate simulated "before vs.
after" performance metrics alongside plain-language engineering rationales. By replacing slow, manual signal retiming
workflows with an intelligent advisory system, MetroFlow AI reduces corridor travel times, decreases vehicle emissions, and
optimizes municipal infrastructure without requiring real-time signal control hardware modifications.
2. Product Vision
To become the global standard for data-driven municipal traffic management, empowering every city to eliminate preventable
gridlock through intelligent, explainable, and accessible signal timing optimization.
3. Mission Statement
Deliver an offline traffic analytics platform that transforms historical vehicle count data into actionable, safe, and simulated
signal timing recommendations, enabling traffic engineers to optimize corridor efficiency within minutes rather than months.
4. Problem Statement
Urban traffic congestion costs cities billions annually in lost productivity, excess fuel consumption, and heightened carbon
emissions. Municipal traffic signal timing plans are traditionally updated only once every 3 to 5 years due to the high cost and
manual labor required for traffic counting, micro-simulation setup (e.g., VISSIM, Synchro), and manual plan generation.
Consequently, signal controllers run static timing programs that do not match current traffic flows, causing artificial gridlock,
excessive vehicle idling, and uncoordinated corridor progression. Municipalities require an offline decision-support tool that
ingests available volume data, identifies peak bottleneck patterns, and recommends optimized green split schedules
accompanied by explainable metrics and safety guardrails.
5. Goals
Automate Bottleneck Detection: Reduce the time required to identify peak traffic congestion windows and corridor
bottlenecks from weeks to under 30 seconds.
Optimize Green Split & Coordination: Generate offline signal timing plans (cycle lengths, green splits, and offsets) that
yield an estimated 12%–25% reduction in average intersection delay based on historical flow data.
Explainable Recommendations: Provide plain-language narrative explanations powered by AI for every recommended
signal change to build engineer trust.

9. Stakeholder Analysis
10. Pain Points
Static Timing Inflexibility: Intersections run static 24-hour plans that fail during unannounced volume surges or seasonal
shifts.
Data Overwhelm vs. Action Gap: Cities collect millions of vehicle count data points from loop detectors and cameras,
but lack tools to automatically synthesize this into timing parameters.
High Retiming Costs: Traditional retiming projects cost $3,000–$5,000 per intersection and take 6–12 months to execute
manually.
Black-Box AI Skepticism: Traffic engineers reject AI systems that provide raw numbers without engineering rationale or
explicit mathematical assumptions.
Complex Modeling Overhead: Existing simulation software requires specialized expertise, long render times, and
manual input parameter mapping.
11. Existing Workflow
Manual Data Collection: Portable pneumatic tubes or historical inductive loop logs record vehicle counts over a 7-day
period.
Data Sanitization: Engineers manually clean corrupted CSVs in Excel.
Model Construction: Data is manually imported and mapped into traditional traffic software (e.g., Synchro/HCS).
Manual Calibration: Engineer manually adjusts phase splits, yellow intervals, and all-red clearance times over several
weeks.
Field Deployment: Approved parameters are manually keyed into physical signal controller cabinets on site.
Post-Evaluation: Minimal or no post-implementation validation occurs due to budget constraints.
12. Proposed Workflow
Data Ingestion: User uploads historical intersection volume logs (CSV/JSON), road metadata (lanes, speed limits, turning
ratios), and optional incident reports into MetroFlow AI.
Automated Analytics: Platform automatically parses counts, identifies peak hourly patterns, computes volume-to-
capacity (V/C) ratios, and highlights specific corridor bottlenecks.

15. Functional Requirements
Module A: Data Ingestion & Processing
FR-A01: Ingest structured CSV/JSON file uploads containing hourly or minute-by-minute vehicle counts per intersection
approach.
FR-A02: Support manual metadata entry/editing for lane configurations, speed limits, free-flow speeds, and turning
movement splits (Left, Through, Right).
FR-A03: Detect and flag corrupted, missing, or outlier data points with options for interpolation or exclusion.
FR-A04: Support optional ingestion of incident logs (accidents, construction events) tagged by timestamp and location.
Module B: Congestion Analytics Engine
FR-B01: Compute Volume-to-Capacity (V/C) ratios for each approach and total intersection per time interval.
FR-B02: Identify peak congestion windows (AM Peak, Midday, PM Peak) automatically using temporal cluster analysis.
FR-B03: Assign Highway Capacity Manual (HCM) Level of Service (LOS) letters (A through F) based on calculated control
delay.
FR-B04: Map multi-intersection bottlenecks along defined linear corridors.
Module C: Signal Timing Recommendation Engine
FR-C01: Calculate optimal green split distribution per signal phase based on Webster’s method and queue saturation
balances.
FR-C02: Calculate recommended total cycle length constrained within user-defined min/max bounds (e.g., 60s to 180s).
FR-C03: Calculate arterial coordination offset values across adjacent intersections to establish green wave progression
velocity.
FR-C04: Enforce hard safety lower-bounds for minimum green, yellow change, and red clearance intervals based on road
geometry and pedestrian walk speeds.
Module D: Queuing Simulation & Validation
FR-D01: Simulate "before vs. after" queue length accumulation and dissipation using M/M/1 and Webster queuing
formulation logic.
FR-D02: Calculate predicted reduction in average control delay per vehicle (sec/veh).
FR-D03: Estimate environmental impact delta (fuel consumption in gallons saved, CO2 emission reduction in kilograms).
FR-D04: Display interactive slider controls allowing users to perform "what-if" traffic volume variation stress testing (+/-
30%).
Module E: AI Rationale & Export Suite
FR-E01: Integrate Azure OpenAI service to generate natural-language explanations for all timing adjustments, detailing
cause-and-effect drivers.
FR-E02: Render explicit listing of all simulation parameters, formulas, and assumptions used during calculation.
FR-E03: Generate downloadable NEMA/170/2070 controller-formatted signal timing sheets (CSV format).
FR-E04: Generate branded, client-ready executive summary reports in PDF format.
16. Non-Functional Requirements
Performance: Processing and timing calculation for up to 20 corridor intersections must complete in under 5 seconds. AI
narrative generation must return in under 3 seconds.
Accuracy & Reliability: Queuing model delay estimations must align within +/- 10% variance against classical Highway
Capacity Manual (HCM) equations under stable flow assumptions.

24. Constraints & Guardrails
Mandatory Operating Guardrails:
Offline Only: The platform MUST NOT issue direct control commands or interface with live signal infrastructure; all
outputs are advisory recommendations.
Explicit Assumptions: All simulation outputs MUST clearly display mathematical models, queuing formulas, and
assumed variables used during computation.
No Guaranteed Claims: The system MUST explicitly avoid marketing or reporting claims that guarantee specific
real-world travel time reductions, attributing all numbers to simulated estimates.
Safety Primacy: Minimum pedestrian walk times and clearance intervals MUST serve as non-negotiable hard
constraints in all optimization routines.
25. Acceptance Criteria
AC-01: System successfully ingests valid multi-intersection CSV traffic count logs and displays clean volume charts
without crashing.
AC-02: System correctly identifies the peak 1-hour traffic volume window for any given 24-hour dataset.
AC-03: Calculated green splits sum exactly to the total defined cycle length minus clearance lost times.
AC-04: Recommended green time for any phase never drops below user-defined minimum pedestrian crossing limits.
AC-05: Simulation module calculates and displays both baseline and optimized average delay (sec/veh) and queue
lengths (meters) side-by-side.
AC-06: Azure OpenAI integration returns a coherent 2-3 paragraph plain-language narrative explaining recommended
green split adjustments.
AC-07: Controller timing export generates a valid, properly formatted CSV file containing phase timing parameters.
26. Competitive Advantage
Traditional incumbents in the traffic engineering space fall into two extremes: expensive, ultra-complex microscopic simulation
suites (e.g., VISSIM, Synchro) that require weeks of manual setup, or expensive, real-time adaptive signal hardware systems
(e.g., SCATS, SCOOT) requiring millions in hardware overhauls. MetroFlow AI bridges this gap by providing an instant, cloud-
native, offline advisory platform that ingests existing volume data, optimizes timing plans via mathematical queuing models,
validates results through lightweight simulation, and provides explainable AI rationales—delivering 80% of the value of high-
end tools at 5% of the time and cost.
27. Product Differentiators
AI-Powered Plain-Language Explanations: Converts complex mathematical split adjustments into executive-ready
narratives that build immediate trust with engineers and city officials.

on IoT road sensors or new controllers.
Instant Queuing Simulation: Eliminates the multi-week model calibration process required by traditional micro-simulators
through automated mathematical queuing validation.
Built-In Safety Guardrails: Hardcodes statutory safety bounds (MUTCD minimum walk times) into the core optimization
algorithm so recommendations are legally and operationally sound.

Critical Evaluation & Judge Review
1. Key Product Weaknesses
Reliance on Static Historical CSVs: The MVP assumes municipal count data is clean and structured. In reality, city loop
detector logs are notoriously messy, corrupted, or contain long gaps.
Simplified Queuing Model Limitations: M/M/1 and Webster queuing models assume steady-state arrivals, which can
under-predict delay during extreme oversaturated conditions (V/C > 1.2) where queues compound exponentially.
Lack of Manual Override Granularity in MVP: Early UI designs focus heavily on automated AI generation, which can
alienate conservative traffic engineers who demand granular control over individual phase clearance intervals.
2. Missing Features
Pedestrian & Bicycle Phasing Constraints: The initial feature set emphasizes vehicular throughput, but urban traffic
engineers must legally accommodate minimum pedestrian walk and flashing don't walk (FDW) intervals.
Corridor Offset Progression Diagrams (Time-Space Diagrams): Traffic engineers rely heavily on Time-Space
diagrams to visualize green wave bands across multi-intersection corridors. This visual was omitted from early functional
requirements.
3. Unnecessary / Over-Engineered Features
Complex LLM Natural Language Chat Interfaces: Adding a conversational chatbot for retiming setup adds unnecessary
complexity and non-deterministic behavior. A structured narrative summary report is far more practical for municipal
compliance.
Real-time API Push Intentions: Attempting to build real-time signal controller integration hooks violates problem
statement guardrails and creates severe municipal security/safety liability concerns.
4. Opportunities to Improve Before UI/UX Design
Integrate HCM Minimum Safety Hard-Bounds: Hardcode statutory minimum pedestrian crossing formulas explicitly into
the optimization algorithm so the UI automatically blocks invalid green split adjustments.
Add Interactive Time-Space Progression Diagrams: Include a visual Time-Space graph on Screen 4 showing green
wave bands along the corridor.
